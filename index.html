Behind the method

What is Gauss–Jordan elimination?

Gauss–Jordan elimination is a systematic way to solve a group of linear equations. It uses three legal row operations—swapping rows, multiplying a row by a nonzero number, and adding a multiple of one row to another—to simplify an augmented matrix.

The process continues until the matrix reaches reduced row echelon form. At that point, a unique answer can be read directly, or the matrix reveals that the system has infinitely many solutions or no solution at all.
